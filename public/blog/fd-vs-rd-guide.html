FD vs RD: which deposit suits you?

Both are safe, fixed-rate bank deposits. The difference is how you put the money in.

The difference

  • Fixed Deposit (FD): you deposit one lump sum and it earns interest until maturity.
  • Recurring Deposit (RD): you deposit a fixed amount every month for the chosen tenure.

Choose FD if you have a lump sum now. Choose RD to build a savings habit from your monthly income.

Why compounding matters

With compounding, interest is added to the deposit and then earns interest itself. Banks usually compound quarterly. The more often interest is added, the higher the maturity value.

Worked example

₹1,00,000 for 5 years at 7% a year, compounded quarterly: maturity is about ₹1,41,478, so the interest is about ₹41,478.

With simple interest the same deposit would give ₹1,35,000, so compounding adds about ₹6,478 here.

Things to remember

  • Interest is taxable. Banks may deduct TDS above a yearly limit, so check the current rules with your bank.
  • Breaking a deposit early usually means a lower rate or a penalty.
  • Look at inflation too. A 7% return with 5% inflation grows your money's buying power by only about 2%.
